# Glossary

## Season

One of four time windows in the year: winter, spring, summer, autumn. The
windows are editorial, chosen by hand rather than taken from the
meteorological or astronomical calendar. Exactly one season is current at any
moment.

## Seasonal scene

The decorative layer shown on the site while a season is current, such as
falling snow, a snowman, or snow resting on text. A seasonal scene sits on top
of the content and of either theme, never replaces them, and can be removed or
swapped for another season's scene without touching the content.

## Theme

The light or dark colour scheme of the site. Independent of the season: every
seasonal scene has to work in both themes.

## Ambient layer

The animated, three-dimensional part of a seasonal scene, drawn behind or
around the content: falling snow, a snowman, trees. It is optional for any
given visit; the site is complete without it.

## Content accents

The still part of a seasonal scene that decorates the content itself, such as
snow resting on the letters of headings. Content accents never reduce
readability and remain when the ambient layer is not running.

## Season control

The visitor-facing switch that turns the whole seasonal scene on or off, both
the ambient layer and the content accents. The choice is remembered across
visits and applies to every season. An explicit choice always wins over what
the site would otherwise pick for the visitor. The control is absent when the
current season has no scene.

## Occasion

A dated stretch inside one season, such as Christmas inside winter, during
which extra elements are added to that season's scene. An occasion belongs to
exactly one season and adds to its scene without replacing it. Occasions in
the same season may overlap.

## Lighter form

The reduced-cost version of an ambient layer: the same composition drawn with
fewer flakes, simpler shadows and a lower frame rate. Phones and less capable
devices get it from the start, and a visit in the full form drops to it when
the full form runs too slowly. The full form and the lighter form ship in the
same files.
